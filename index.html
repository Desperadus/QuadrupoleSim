The Oscillating Quadrupole Field

Four parallel rods surround the axis. One opposing pair is held at +Φ₀, the other at −Φ₀, where the applied potential mixes a DC offset with a radio-frequency drive:

Φ₀(t) = U − V · cos(Ωt)

For ideal hyperbolic rods the potential inside is a pure two-dimensional saddle that has no component along the axis:

Φ(x, y, t) = Φ₀(t) · (x2 − y2) / r₀2

Because the potential is independent of z, an ion coasts along the axis at whatever speed the source gave it. The quadrupole never accelerates it forwards — it only decides whether it survives the trip.

Equations of Motion → Mathieu

Differentiating the potential gives a restoring force in one transverse direction and a repelling force in the other, swapping roles every half RF cycle:

m · d2x/dt2 = −2Q(U − V cos Ωt) · x / r₀2
m · d2y/dt2 = +2Q(U − V cos Ωt) · y / r₀2

Substituting ξ = Ωt/2 turns both into the Mathieu equation:

d2u/dξ2 + (au − 2qu cos 2ξ) u = 0
ax = 8QU / (mΩ2r₀2)     qx = 4QV / (mΩ2r₀2)     ay = −ax, qy = −qx

Critical note: the charge Q appears in the numerator and inside m in the denominator, so both parameters depend only on the ratio m/z. A doubly charged ion of mass 1000 behaves exactly like a singly charged ion of mass 500. That is why a quadrupole is a mass-to-charge filter and not a mass filter.

The Stability Diagram

The Mathieu equation has bounded solutions only for certain (a, q). An ion survives only if its motion is bounded in both x and y at once. Because ay = −ax, the joint region is symmetric about a = 0 and forms the familiar triangle. This simulation finds it by Floquet analysis rather than from a table — it integrates one RF period, builds the monodromy matrix and checks |trace| < 2. The apex and the RF-only cutoff below are values the program derived, not values it was given.

Scanning: The Operating Line

Both a and q are inversely proportional to m/z, so at a fixed U and V every ion sits at a different point along the ray a = (2U/V) · q, ordered by mass. Only the slice of that ray inside the triangle is transmitted. Ramping U and V together keeps the ray fixed and sweeps the mass scale past it — that is a mass scan.

  • Steeper line (U/V closer to the apex): a shorter segment inside the triangle, so a narrower Δm and higher resolution — but a much smaller acceptance, so fewer ions survive. This is the fundamental resolution/transmission trade-off of every quadrupole.
  • Constant Δm operation: because Δm/m is set by the line slope, a fixed slope gives resolution proportional to mass. Real instruments adjust U/V during the scan to hold Δm constant instead.

RF-Only Mode

Setting U = 0 puts the operating line along the q axis. Every ion is then stable up to the cutoff q where the triangle closes, which makes the device a high-pass mass filter: light ions are ejected, everything heavier is guided through. RF-only quadrupoles are used as ion guides, collision cells and pre-filters rather than as analysers.

Ions in an RF-only guide feel a time-averaged effective potential — the pseudopotential — that grows with the square of the displacement from the axis, which is what confines them despite the field itself being defocusing half the time.

Real Rods Are Round

Hyperbolic rods are expensive to machine, so most instruments use round rods. A round-rod set cannot produce a pure quadrupole field; the potential picks up higher harmonics that the four-fold symmetry allows:

Φ(x, y) = Φ₀ · Σn = 2, 6, 10 … An · Re[((x + iy)/r₀)n]

This simulator solves the two-dimensional boundary value problem for the actual rod surfaces with the charge simulation method and reads the harmonics off by Fourier analysis, so changing r/r₀ genuinely changes the field. Sweep the ratio in the Setup tab and watch the 12-pole coefficient A₆ pass through zero — that null is the reason a particular rod radius is chosen.

Fringing Fields

The field cannot switch on instantly at the entrance. In the fringe region the transverse field ramps up and, because the potential now varies with z, an axial component appears:

Ez = −Φ₀(t) · s′(z) · (x2 − y2) / r₀2

An ion crossing this region at an unlucky RF phase gets a transverse kick that can throw it out of the acceptance even though its (a, q) is deep inside the stable triangle. Compare transmission with the fringe on and off to see the cost.

What This Device Is Not

A quadrupole mass filter is entirely electrostatic and radio-frequency. There is no magnetic field anywhere in it. Magnetic analysers (sector instruments) and ion cyclotron resonance cells separate ions with a magnetic field; a quadrupole separates them purely through the stability of a driven oscillator.
